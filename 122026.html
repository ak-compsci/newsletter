NEW FROM DEED

AI Ready Skills: Free AI Curriculum for Every Alaska School Serving Grades 6-12

Grades 6-12 | Curriculum, credentials, and teacher training | Free through DEED | Teachers and administrators

Through a statewide license from DEED, every Alaska school serving grades 6 through 12 now has access to AI Ready Skills, an AI literacy program from Prodigy Learning. The curriculum, assessments, student credentials, and teacher training are free to districts.

Students learn how AI works and how to use it responsibly in four game-based units: Introduction to AI, Exploring Data and AI, Coding and AI, and Practicing Responsible AI. They earn a credential for each unit, then take a Capstone Credential exam they can show to colleges and employers. The program is aligned to the Alaska computer science standards and has earned the ISTE Seal.

Teachers don't need any AI experience. An optional six-hour Certified Educator course prepares them to teach the full program, live online, self-paced, or in person.

Webinar for Alaska educators: a 30-minute overview to get started, Friday, December 11, at 3:00 PM Alaska time.

The Hour of AI and Hour of Code, December 7-13

K-12 | One hour | Free | Educators and students

The new Hour of AI activities are live for CS Education Week. This year, students build something of their own.

Each one gives students a start on learning to understand, direct, question, and create with the technology shaping their world.

Spotlight on Alaska

Wrangell Teen Wins Red Nation Student Film Award for Totem Pole Documentary

Wrangell | Student film, 12 minutes | Educators, students, and families

Image: Stikine Stories

Quinton "Quinn" Davies, a 14-year-old Tlingit filmmaker from Wrangell, won the Student Film Award at the Red Nation International Film Festival in Los Angeles. His film, Standing Tall in Wrangell, documents the July 2025 raising of five totem poles, the first raised in Wrangell in 38 years, and follows carver Aak'wtaatseen Mike Hoyt. It was one of five student films, out of 17, that screened in person on November 13.

Davies edited the film in iMovie on an iPad in eighth grade, working with drone footage and photographs. He made it through Stikine Stories, a youth film program run by his mother and teacher, Laura Davies. His advice to young filmmakers in small communities: "start small and talk to people and get out and start filming."

Curriculum Corner

New AI Lessons for Grades 6-12 from aiEDU

Grades 6-12 | Short lessons and multi-day units | Free account | Educators in any subject

aiEDU released AI Foundations, a multi-day Learning Journey for grades 6-8. It opens with whether a "smart" thermostat counts as AI. Students then work through cases from self-driving cars to AI search answers to learn where an AI tool's behavior comes from and when to trust it. The eight resources include slides, teacher guides, a student workbook, assessments, and a one-page Quick Start Guide, and they fit social studies, math, science, ELA, or computer science.

For less class time, Intro to AI Snapshots for grades 7-12 has two activities, each under 30 minutes, on AI concepts and robotics. AI Foundations builds on these Snapshots. Downloads need a free aiEDU account.

Students Build Their Own AI Apps with Playlab's AI Agency Curriculum

Secondary | Adaptable curriculum | Free curriculum, paid Playlab app | Educators

Playlab's Student AI Agency curriculum coaches students to design, build, and test their own AI applications to solve problems in their communities. Along the way they learn how AI works and how to evaluate and critique AI systems. The package also shows teachers how to submit student projects to national competitions with cash prizes. Students build their apps in Playlab, which is a paid platform.

Last year, students at Robinson Secondary School built Civic Lens, which compares how different outlets cover the same story, down to framing, tone, and word choice. It won the ISTE+ASCD and Presidential AI Challenge competitions.

Spot the Scam with Click It or Skip It from Common Sense Media

Grades 3-12 | Classroom game | No login | Educators

In Common Sense Media's new game, students read, watch, or listen to a message and decide whether it's safe to engage with (click it) or a scam to avoid (skip it). The scenarios cover online scams, AI-powered deception, and social engineering. Each round has 10 scenarios, timed or untimed, and is built for a shared screen with the teacher leading.

The game is aligned to ISTE, CSTA, and CYBER.ORG standards and collects no student names. It's also in the Hour of AI catalog.

Arduino for Beginners, Free Online or with Hardware

Beginners | 8 lessons, 90 minutes each | Free and open source | Teachers, librarians, and clubs

This free curriculum takes beginners from blinking a single LED to buttons, sensors, and servos, ending with two open-ended capstone projects. Each lesson has a lesson plan, code examples, and schematics. No electronics experience is needed.

Presidential SI Challenge: Register by December 18

K-12 students and educators | Team project | Free to enter | Teams led by a teacher or community group leader

Register by Friday, December 18. Submit projects by Wednesday, December 23. Both deadlines are 1:00 PM Alaska time. Teams that miss registration can't submit.

Last year's Presidential AI Challenge is back under a new name. The White House now calls it the Presidential Super Intelligence (SI) Challenge, but the projects work with the same artificial intelligence tools students and teachers already know.

Student teams use AI to take on a problem in their own community. Elementary teams have up to five students and middle and high school teams up to four, each led by an adult 21 or older, such as a teacher or a 4-H, FFA, or scout leader. Teams submit a written project of up to 10 pages and a video of up to four minutes. K-12 teachers can also enter in an educator category, in teams of one to three, with a project on how they teach with or build AI.

State Champions receive $200 gift cards. Regional Champions travel to Washington, DC, and National Champions win up to $12,500. Participants must be U.S. citizens, nationals, or permanent residents. Tribally controlled schools get an extra state-level champion in each category.

NASA Moon Base Challenge Offers Up to $1 Million to Student Teams

Grades 9-12 and college | Team project | Free | Register by January 25, 2027

Image: [CREDIT TK]

NASA is offering up to $1 million in prizes for student designs that would help astronauts live and work on the Moon. Teams of two to five students, with an adult coach such as a teacher, pick one of six categories: power, building with lunar materials, life support, surface mobility, communication networks, or astronaut safety. High school teams compete in their own division.

Teams first submit a five- to seven-page concept paper by February 22. Up to 100 finalist teams earn awards and move on to a pitch video and slide deck. Students can use AI but must disclose it. Teams must register on NASA's STEM Gateway by January 25.

What the Research Says About AI in K-12: Stanford's November Update

Administrators | Research review

Stanford's SCALE Initiative updated its review of causal research on generative AI in education, meaning randomized trials and quasi-experimental studies. The evidence base grew from 20 papers in March to 52. Five of the new student studies took place in U.S. K-12 settings; the March review had none.

AI appears more likely to help when students use it to understand material than when it produces the work for them, though neither use consistently improved learning. Students often reported a better experience without learning more. AI saved teachers time on lesson planning, and teachers given AI drafts gave more and better feedback in the same amount of time. Some tools also undermined student motivation.

The authors note that most of the studies are preprints that haven't been peer-reviewed, most come from colleges, and none yet show long-term learning gains. If your district is weighing an AI tool, the review is a check on what its vendor claims.
