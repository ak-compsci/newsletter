Spotlight on Alaska

Statewide Students Shape AI Guidance

Photo source: The 74 Million

Students from all 50 states recently came together through Day of AI to draft and debate the STUDENTS FIRST Act of 2026, a student-led framework for the responsible use of AI in K-12 schools.

For Alaska, the story also includes Kenai student Chase Johnson, who told The 74 that AI can help with personal projects and business ideas, but students still need to do the hard thinking themselves.

That makes this a statewide feature worth following: it highlights student voice, practical AI literacy, and the questions Alaska students are already asking about how the technology should be used in school.

Read the full story at The 74 | Visit Day of AI.

Bering Strait School District Chose Another Path

KNOM's first story in a three-part series on Bering Strait School District's secondary ELA work starts with the real problem: the national curriculum options did not fit the district's students or communities.

The district reviewed national programs, worked with teachers, parents, and community members, and kept coming back to the same conclusion. The materials were expensive, hard to sustain, and not grounded enough in the lives of rural Alaska students, so the district chose to build its own curriculum instead.

That work has meant a lot of revising, listening, and fixing what did not work. It also shows a practical way to merge instructional content with stakeholder feedback and AI-era thinking: start with community needs, then design the curriculum around them.

Read Part 1 from KNOM and follow along for Parts 2 and 3 as the series continues.

Mount Edgecumbe Students Use Technology to Support Their School and Community

Photo credit: T3 Alaska Program

The Mount Edgecumbe T3 Alaska story shows students using 3D printing, Raspberry Pi projects, energy work, and design thinking to meet real needs at school and in the community.

Students created fidget toys and replacement parts, prototyped 3D-printed settling plates with the Sitka Sound Science Center, built practical tools for classrooms, and even explored wind turbine design and energy efficiency.

It is a strong statewide example of learning by doing and a useful feature for readers looking for Alaska-made STEM and CS connections.

Curriculum Corner

This Robot Speaks Endangered Native American Languages

Photo credit: Steam Connection

NPR recently highlighted a robot designed to speak endangered Native American languages, with a particular focus on the SkoBot project and the broader work around language preservation and cultural continuity.

The companion site, Find more information about the SkoBot and ways to get involved, includes additional project details. One point worth carrying into Alaska conversations is the role of data sovereignty in protecting language, culture, and community control over knowledge.

This pairs well with the statewide AI conversation above by showing how Alaska educators and communities are thinking about language, culture, and technology together.

The AI Collective: Privacy, Safety, and Tool Reviews

The AI Collective helps educators, administrators, and technology leaders think through privacy, data practices, and environmental considerations for AI tools commonly used in K-12 settings.

The practical value here is straightforward: you can search for a specific tool, read a full report, and make a more informed decision before teacher or student use.

If your district is building an approval or guidance process, this is a useful starting point.

CTE Clusters and AI Integration

The resource Responsive By Design: Integrating Artificial Intelligence Across the National Career Clusters Framework offers concrete examples of how AI is already used across the 14 Career Clusters and gives state leaders, educators, and partners a way to think about AI literacy as a transferable skill.

For CTE planning, the brief is a useful conversation starter for aligning programs with industry needs, building statewide guidance or standards, and designing experiences that connect AI to real career pathways.

It also gives core academic educators a path for weaving AI literacy into everyday instruction so students can build skills that carry across subjects, credentials, and future work.

Note: The TechAccess: AI-Ready America NSF grant will support initiatives to enhance AI literacy and integration in education in every state. Keep an eye on the AK awarded grantee and ways you and your students can be involved.

Upcoming Events

Alaska Science Olympiad Registration Is Open

Alaska Science Olympiad is a statewide hands-on STEM competition organized at the University of Alaska Fairbanks by Alaska EPSCoR, the UAF College of Engineering and Mines, the UAF College of Natural Science and Mathematics, and the UAF Makerspace.

Registration is open for the 2027 season, with teams committing to compete at UAF in Fairbanks on April 3, 2027. Division B teams can have up to 15 students, and after-school clubs or programs are eligible to register.

For schools looking to plug into a strong statewide EPSCoR effort based in UAF, this is a direct way to get students involved in problem-solving, engineering design, and team-based STEM work.

Congressional App Challenge — Deadline October 26

Registration is open. Middle and high school students build an app in any language and submit it with a demo video. Students can compete individually or in teams of up to four.

Submissions close October 26, 2026 at 12:00 PM ET, and winners are recognized by their member of Congress.

NCWIT Aspirations in Computing — Applications Open September 1

The AiC High School Award recognizes 9th-12th grade students for computing-related achievement and interest. Applications for the 2026-27 season open September 1, 2026 and close October 27, 2026.

Applicants join the AiC Community first, then apply. It is a low-lift nomination that can place Alaska students in front of a national network of scholarships and internships.

Toshiba America Foundation Grants for K-12 Educators

Toshiba America Foundation offers grants for K-12 educators who want to bring project-based STEM learning into their classrooms. The foundation generally does not fund requests solely for equipment, but it does support innovative instruction that helps students build new skills.

K-5 educators can apply for $1,000 grants with an October 1 deadline, and 6-12 educators can apply for $5,000 or more on a rolling basis.

Feature Quote: Why the Work Still Matters

The throughline across these resources is not that AI replaces foundational learning. It is that teachers and students need stronger habits of mind, clearer evaluation skills, and more intentional guidance than ever.

That makes this a good month to revisit how your school introduces AI, how students are taught to question outputs, and where CS and digital literacy already support those goals.
