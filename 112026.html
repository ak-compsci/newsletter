For district IT: patch PHP now

On October 21, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) urged organizations to immediately patch two critical flaws in PHP, known together as "php2shell." The flaws let an attacker take control of a web server running PHP without logging in. On October 26, CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, which means attackers are already using them.

PHP runs many school websites and is often built into other software, so it may be on systems you don't think of as PHP servers. CISA's guidance:

  • Inventory every system running PHP, starting with internet-facing ones.
  • Update to PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, or 8.2.35.
  • Take systems offline if they can't be updated right away or still run PHP 8.1 or earlier.
  • Watch for PHP worker processes launching shells or downloading tools.

If your district doesn't have IT staff to do this, ask your website and software vendors whether they have patched.

The Hour of AI, December 7-13

K-12 | One hour | Free | Educators and students

Your students use AI every day. The Hour of AI gives them an hour to find out how it works. (That goes for you, too.)

The Hour of AI comes from the makers of the Hour of Code and runs during CS Education Week. Activities come from Code.org, Google, Microsoft, Minecraft, LEGO Education, the Scratch Foundation, and others. Keep at it, and that becomes digital fluency: the ability to understand, direct, question, and create with the technology shaping students' world.

Grades 6-12 and families | Self-paced | Free | Students, educators, and families

Students on NAMLE's Youth Advisory Council built two new resources. The Feed Effect explains how online behavior, data collection, and algorithms decide what shows up in a feed. It includes student-written blog posts, a zine, and an interview with a former Meta employee.

For Leaders

What Counts as AI Education? A New Framework from Computing Researchers

Administrators | 15-minute read

The Computing Research Association, working with the CSforALL Alliance, ACM, and CSTA, released a framework in August that defines AI literacy and AI education and how each relates to computer science. Its argument is that AI literacy needs a computer science foundation but can't replace it. Teaching only tool use leaves students able to operate AI but not to build or evaluate it.

If your district is writing AI guidance or choosing an AI literacy curriculum, the definitions give a board or committee shared terms to work from. DEED's AI guidance for districts has a model board policy and a vendor checklist to pair with it.
